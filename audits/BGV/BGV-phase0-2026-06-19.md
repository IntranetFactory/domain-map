The compliance machinery is the heaviest part of this domain: a single order can spawn a disclosure, an authorization, a Summary-of-Rights acknowledgement, a pre-adverse notice, an adverse notice, and a dispute, each an auditable row under FCRA, before any EEOC individualized assessment or state ban-the-box layer.

## Screening machinery (the orderable shape)

- `background_check_packages` is the orderable bundle/catalog template the buyer selects.
- `background_check_components` are the individual searches a package expands into (county criminal, federal criminal, national, education, employment, MVR, credit, drug). Component-typed entities (`criminal_record_searches`, `employment_verifications`, `education_verifications`, `motor_vehicle_record_checks`, `drug_health_screenings`, `global_watchlist_searches`) are the concrete search results.
- `background_check_adjudications` is the pass / fail / review decision evaluated against `adjudication_matrices` (the rule set / eligibility matrix). Checkr, Sterling, HireRight, First Advantage and Accurate all expose this as named tooling.
- `continuous_monitoring_subscriptions`, `rescreenings`, `criminal_records_monitoring_alerts`, `identity_verifications` and `right_to_work_verifications` are the post-hire / ongoing layer.

## Modularization hypothesis

Recommended split: **three modules**. The two-module collapse is tempting but the vendor packaging evidence argues against folding continuous/identity into the order flow.

**Module A "Screening Orders & Packages"** scopes the order itself (`background_checks`), the subject (`screening_candidates`), the catalog (`background_check_packages`, `background_check_components`), the concrete searches (criminal, employment, education, MVR, drug/health, credential, watchlist, healthcare exclusion), status history, and candidate-facing invitation/portal/notification engagement. This is the point-in-time transaction every vendor sells first.

**Module B "Adjudication & Compliance"** scopes the decision and the FCRA/EEOC paperwork: `background_check_adjudications`, `adjudication_matrices`, `fcra_disclosures`, `candidate_authorizations`, `pre_adverse_action_notices`, `adverse_action_notices`, `background_check_disputes`, `fcra_summary_of_rights_acknowledgements`, `individualized_assessments`, `ban_the_box_assessments`.

**Module C "Continuous & Identity"** scopes the post-hire and standalone-identity surface: `continuous_monitoring_subscriptions`, `rescreenings`, `criminal_records_monitoring_alerts`, `identity_verifications`, `right_to_work_verifications`, `international_screenings`, `gdpr_consent_records`.

Why three, not two, and why C is its own module: **every named vendor sells post-hire monitoring as a separately-priced, separately-named product**, distinct from the pre-hire order. Checkr packages it as "Continuous Check" / "Continuous Crim" plus time-interval Subscriptions, explicitly contrasted with its "point-in-time" reports. Sterling sells "Sterling Monitoring" as a standalone post-hire product. First Advantage sells "Criminal Records Monitoring" as its own solution page, and HireRight runs continuous programs tied to separate global data feeds. Accurate explicitly frames continuous monitoring as the alternative to re-running full packages. Certn leads with OneID identity verification and ongoing risk monitoring as products in their own right. That uniform separate-product packaging, plus identity verification being sold standalone (Certn OneID, First Advantage RightID, Sterling identity solutions), is the structural signal that the continuous/identity surface is not a tail of the order flow and earns its own module.

Why B stays separate from A even though vendors bundle adjudication into the ordering UI: adjudication and the FCRA adverse-action chain are exposed as their own named tooling by every vendor (HireRight "Adjudication" service, Sterling's adjudication + individualized-assessment tools, First Advantage's "enterprise adjudication engine"), and the compliance entities are regulation-driven (they must exist even where a vendor hides them), so they form a coherent, independently-heavy module rather than fields on the order. A two-module split (A = Orders+Packages, B = Adjudication+Compliance, continuous/identity folded in) is defensible if module count must be minimized, but it buries a layer that every vendor sells as a distinct SKU. Recommendation: three modules.
