## Modularization hypothesis

Five modules, each with >=1 genuine TPRM master:

1. **Third-Party Onboarding and Intake** (masters: `third_parties`, `third_party_engagements`, `third_party_tiers`, `third_party_contacts`, `onboarding_requests`, `offboarding_records`, `critical_function_designations`; consumes `suppliers`, `contracts`). The entry point: register the broad third party, scope engagements, classify criticality. Vendor evidence: Archer separates Catalog/Engagement; ServiceNow Tiering is a first-class step.
2. **Due Diligence and Assessment** (masters: `third_party_assessments`, `due_diligence_questionnaires`, `assessment_responses`, `assessment_campaigns`, `vendor_documents`, plus all BAA/DPA/attestation/PCI/oversight compliance docs; consumes GRC `compliance_controls`/`control_assessments` and `risk_categories`). The deep, contract-gated diligence that distinguishes TPRM from SRM's qualification-gated assessment. Vendor evidence: Venminder Questionnaires + Due Diligence; Prevalent 800+ templates.
3. **Continuous Risk Monitoring** (masters: `third_party_risk_ratings`, `security_ratings`, `monitoring_signals`). Post-contract watching: ingest external ratings/breach signals, recompute internal score, drive re-assessment cadence. Vendor evidence: Ven-monitor, Prevalent Monitoring, BitSight/SecurityScorecard feeds.
4. **Remediation and Issue Management** (masters: `third_party_risk_findings`, `third_party_remediation_plans`, `risk_acceptances`). Findings into tracked remediation closing before renewal; exceptions/acceptances with expiry. Vendor evidence: ServiceNow Issues and Remediations; Archer findings-as-exceptions plus remediation plans.
5. **Supply-Chain (Nth-Party) Risk** (masters: `fourth_party_relationships`, `concentration_risk_views`, `ict_provider_registers`, `subcontracting_chains`, `supply_chain_attestations`). Subcontractor visibility, concentration risk, the DORA register. Vendor evidence: UpGuard supply-chain mapping, Archer fourth-party category, DORA RTS.

## SRM-vs-TPRM boundary verdict

**TPRM masters a broad `third_parties` record and CONSUMES `suppliers` from SRM. Suppliers are a subtype/source, not a re-master.** Single-master integrity (M7) holds: `suppliers`, `supplier_risk_assessments`, and `supplier_certifications` keep their sole master in SRM (28); TPRM never carries a `role='master'` row for them. `third_party_assessments` is a genuinely distinct master from SRM `supplier_risk_assessments`: SRM's is qualification-gated and operational (will this supplier perform), TPRM's is engagement/contract-gated deep diligence (is it safe to sign, what is the tier-driven cadence). The names already differ, so no collision.

Vendor evidence for the supplier-vs-third-party line: every flagship treats the master as the broad "third party," not "supplier." Archer's Third Party Catalog explicitly documents "all third-party relationships, engagements and associated contracts" across business units, and its risk categories span information security, fourth-party, reputation, resiliency — far beyond procurement suppliers. OneTrust's scope is "third parties" for ethics, privacy, and security risk, including processors and data recipients that are not purchase-order suppliers. ProcessUnity/CyberGRX curate 360,000 vendor risk profiles keyed on the third party, not a buyer's supplier master. BitSight/SecurityScorecard rate any internet-facing entity (partners, fintech/API providers, acquisition targets) with no procurement relationship at all. This confirms TPRM's master is broader than SRM's: a supplier is one kind of third party, and TPRM embeds/links the SRM `suppliers` identity rather than duplicating it.

Naming collisions to avoid at load: GRC owns `remediation_plans`, `risk_assessments`, `control_assessments`; some monitoring domain owns `monitoring_alerts`/`monitoring_events`/`monitoring_policies`. TPRM therefore uses `third_party_remediation_plans`, `third_party_assessments`, `monitoring_signals` (not bare `monitoring_alerts`). The `attestation_records` master is distinct from GRC `policy_attestations` (employee-policy attestations) and SRM `supplier_certifications` (it tracks vendor SOC2/ISO report receipt and expiry).

## Open questions for the user

1. Does TPRM master a broad `third_parties` and CONSUME SRM `suppliers` (recommended), or should it embed `suppliers` as an `embedded_master` shell so the module is installable without SRM present? The matrix assumes consume.
2. Five modules or four? Supply-Chain (Nth-Party) is regulation-driven (DORA) and has 5 masters, but if DORA scope is deferred it could fold into Continuous Monitoring, dropping to four modules. Keep it separate?
3. Is `security_ratings` a TPRM master (ingested snapshot, recommended) or a consumed external feed with no local master row? It carries real fields (score, vendor source, scan date), arguing for master.
4. The DORA `ict_provider_registers` / `subcontracting_chains`: confirm TPRM owns the third-party register. It is distinct from any GRC enterprise risk register, but please confirm it is not intended to live in a future dedicated operational-resilience domain.
5. Should `risk_categories` be a TPRM-owned config master or strictly consumed from GRC's risk taxonomy? Flagships ship their own category lists (Archer's 10 residual-risk categories), which argues for a local config master that maps to GRC.
6. Where does the go/no-go pre-contract decision live: a state on `third_party_assessments`, or a separate `risk_decisions` master? The matrix keeps it as an assessment state to avoid a thin master.
7. `contracts` are consumed from CLM (TPRM gates, does not master). Confirm CLM exists/will exist as the contract master, or whether TPRM needs a thin `engagement_contract_refs` link instead.
