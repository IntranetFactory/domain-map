# GRC (Governance, Risk & Compliance) — Phase 0 Vendor-Surface Research

Date: 2026-06-13
Scope: market research for a software-market catalog. Web-grounded vendor analysis. No database, no `semantius`, no MCP writes.

Current GRC catalog footprint (10 mastered objects): compliance_risks, risk_assessments, compliance_controls, control_assessments, compliance_obligations, compliance_evidence, audit_issues, remediation_plans, compliance_policies, policy_attestations.

## 1. Flagship vendors

The recognized GRC / Integrated Risk Management (IRM) leaders. The 4-5 true leaders are marked (LEADER); the rest are strong named players included for surface coverage.

- **ServiceNow Integrated Risk Management (IRM)** (LEADER) — GRC built on the Now Platform. Packaged as four products: Risk Management, Policy and Compliance Management, Audit Management, and Third-Party Risk Management. Known for workflow automation and embedding control testing into operational processes.
- **Archer Suite** (formerly RSA Archer) (LEADER) — the long-standing enterprise GRC platform. Organized into solution areas (Audit, Compliance, Enterprise/Operational Risk, Policy, Issues, Business Continuity, Third-Party, IT/Security Risk) each delivered as configurable "use cases." Deepest legacy footprint in large financial institutions.
- **MetricStream Connected GRC** (LEADER) — enterprise GRC for large, multi-jurisdiction programs. Sold as named apps across three product lines (BusinessGRC, CyberGRC, ESGRC): Enterprise/Operational Risk, Regulatory Compliance, Policy, Internal Audit, Regulatory Change, Case & Incident, Third-Party Risk.
- **OneTrust GRC (Tech Risk & Compliance)** (LEADER) — risk/compliance/audit alongside its privacy heritage. Modules: Risk Management, Compliance/Controls, Policy Management (draft, distribute, attest), Audit Management. Strong on policy attestation and control automation.
- **AuditBoard** (LEADER, compliance/audit SPECIALIST) — connected-risk platform born from internal audit and SOX. Named products: SOXHUB, OpsAudit (operational audit), RiskOversight (ERM), CrossComply (multi-framework IT compliance), TPRM, ESG. The clearest audit-first packaging in the market.
- **Workiva** (compliance/audit + disclosure SPECIALIST) — connected reporting, controls, and disclosure/filing platform. Strong in SOX, internal controls, audit, and regulatory/financial disclosure filings (the disclosure-management angle is distinctive).
- **Riskonnect Connected GRC** — modular GRC spanning ERM, Compliance, IT Risk, Policy Management, Internal Audit, and Third-Party Risk; "start with ERM + Compliance, expand into audit/TPRM" packaging.
- **LogicGate Risk Cloud** — application-based, highly configurable risk platform; ships GRC "Applications" (e.g. Enterprise Risk, Compliance, Policy, Third-Party, Controls Compliance) on a shared workflow engine.
- **Diligent One Platform** (Diligent / former Galvanize-HighBond) — GRC + board governance; modules for audit, risk, compliance, policy, and board/entity management.
- **MEGA HOPEX** — enterprise architecture + IRM; risk, control, and compliance modeling tied to EA repositories.

Compliance/audit specialists called out: **AuditBoard** and **Workiva** (audit, SOX, controls; Workiva adds disclosure filing).

## 3. Modularization hypothesis (KEY DELIVERABLE)

How the flagship leaders package GRC into named modules / products / editions:

- **ServiceNow IRM** — four products: **Risk Management**, **Policy and Compliance Management** (one product combining policy + compliance), **Audit Management**, **Third-Party Risk Management**. Note: policy and compliance are deliberately co-packaged in a single product; audit is its own product.
- **Archer** — solution areas, each a configurable use case: **Issues Management** (foundation, cross-source), **Policy Program Management**, **Regulatory & Corporate Compliance Management**, **Audit Management**, **Enterprise/Operational Risk Management**. Policy and compliance are distinct use cases here, but Issues is its own foundational layer.
- **MetricStream** — named apps grouped under BusinessGRC: **Enterprise Risk** + **Operational Risk**, **Regulatory Compliance**, **Policy**, **Internal Audit**, plus Regulatory Change and Case/Incident. Policy is a standalone app distinct from Compliance and from Audit.
- **OneTrust GRC** — **Risk Management**, **Compliance / Controls**, **Policy Management**, **Audit Management**. Four named modules; policy is standalone, audit standalone.
- **Riskonnect** — **ERM**, **Compliance**, **IT Risk**, **Policy Management**, **Internal Audit**, **Third-Party Risk**. Policy and Audit are each standalone modules.
- **AuditBoard** — product-named, audit-first: **RiskOversight** (risk), **CrossComply** (compliance), **SOXHUB** (SOX/controls), **OpsAudit** (audit), **TPRM**. Compliance and audit are distinct products; policy lives inside the shared connected-risk data core rather than as a separately sold module.

### Verdict on the candidate splits

**Winner: (a) the 3-module split — Risk Management / Compliance Management / Policy & Attestation — is the closest single mirror of actual vendor packaging, but with one important caveat (see below).**

Evidence for the three top-level axes being Risk, Compliance, Policy:
- Archer ships **Policy Program Management** as a use case distinct from **Regulatory & Corporate Compliance Management** and from **Enterprise/Operational Risk Management** — three separate axes.
- MetricStream ships **Policy** as a standalone app distinct from **Regulatory Compliance** and from **Enterprise Risk**.
- OneTrust and Riskonnect each list **Policy Management** as its own module separate from Risk and from Compliance.

This rules out split (b) (Risk / combined Compliance-and-Policy): only ServiceNow co-packages policy with compliance (its "Policy and Compliance Management" product). The majority of leaders (Archer, MetricStream, OneTrust, Riskonnect) keep Policy as its own named module, so a merged Compliance-and-Policy module contradicts the dominant packaging.

On split (c) (4-module: Risk / Compliance / Policy / Evidence-and-Audit): there is real vendor support for audit being its own module — ServiceNow **Audit Management**, MetricStream **Internal Audit**, OneTrust **Audit Management**, Riskonnect **Internal Audit**, and AuditBoard's entire identity (**OpsAudit** as a named product). However, no flagship packages "Evidence" as a standalone sold module: evidence is a shared object consumed by audit and compliance, never sold separately. So the clean 4th module is **Audit**, not "Evidence-and-Audit."

**Refined verdict:** the truest mirror of flagship packaging is a 3-module core (Risk Management / Compliance Management / Policy & Attestation) **with Audit as a strong fourth module** when an audit program is in scope. In other words: (a) is correct for the GRC core, and a 4-module variant is defensible if and only if the fourth module is named **Audit** (not Evidence). Evidence should remain a cross-cutting object inside both Compliance and Audit, mirroring AuditBoard's "unified data core" and ServiceNow's shared evidence store. Do not split on Evidence; do consider splitting out Audit.

Concrete naming support: ServiceNow "Audit Management," MetricStream "Internal Audit," OneTrust "Audit Management," Riskonnect "Internal Audit," AuditBoard "OpsAudit" all justify an Audit module by name; Archer "Policy Program Management," MetricStream "Policy," OneTrust "Policy Management," Riskonnect "Policy Management" all justify Policy as its own module by name.

## 4. Capability ownership: Compliance Training

**Verdict: GRC flagships do NOT own training content delivery. They own the policy attestation / acknowledgement record and consume a training-completion record as evidence. Compliance Training delivery belongs to LMS; GRC should keep only the attestation/completion link, not a training-authoring/delivery capability.**

Evidence:
- The GRC primitive across vendors is **attestation/acknowledgement**, not course delivery. OneTrust Policy Management explicitly covers "documentation, distribution, **attestation**, and management of policies" and tracks policy attestations with automated follow-up. The object is the attestation record, not the courseware.
- ServiceNow Policy and Compliance Management generates Issues from **attestation results** and control-test results: it ingests attestation/completion outcomes as compliance signals (evidence), rather than authoring or delivering training.
- Archer Policy Program Management manages policy distribution and acknowledgement; training delivery itself is out of band.
- None of the flagship product catalogs (ServiceNow IRM's four products, Archer's solution areas, MetricStream's apps, OneTrust's modules, AuditBoard's products) lists a "training delivery" or "courseware/LMS" module. Course authoring, enrollment, SCORM/xAPI content, and completion tracking are LMS concerns.

Implication for the catalog: GRC should master the **attestation/acknowledgement** record (already does, as policy_attestations) and treat **training completion** as an inbound evidence signal from an LMS. A "Compliance Training" capability that implies content authoring/delivery should be handed to LMS; GRC keeps the link only as "consumes training-completion as attestation evidence."

## 5. audit_issues vs audit_findings ownership

**Verdict: the market is split, and the split is meaningful. Audit specialists keep a distinct audit Finding/Observation object upstream of a shared, cross-domain Issue; workflow-platform vendors (ServiceNow, OneTrust) merge findings into a single Issue object.**

Who splits (distinct Finding/Observation, separate from Issue):
- **AuditBoard** — audit-first products (OpsAudit/SOXHUB) generate audit **findings/observations** during fieldwork, which then surface alongside business and IT tickets in a unified remediation view. Finding is an audit-program artifact; Issue is the cross-source remediation item.
- **Workiva** — audit and controls workflows track findings/observations distinctly within the audit/controls layer before they roll into remediation.
- **Archer** — keeps **Issues Management** as a separate foundational use case that consolidates issues "generated by multiple groups such as audit, risk, and compliance," while audit findings/observations live inside **Audit Management**. So Archer has both a distinct audit finding and a separate cross-domain Issue — the clearest two-object model.

Who merges (one Issue object, findings fold in):
- **ServiceNow** — has a single **Issue** object. Issues are "automatically generated from indicator results, attestation results, or control test effectiveness," and failed controls "automatically generate Issue records." There is no separate persisted "audit finding" master; a finding is realized directly as an Issue, then driven by Remediation Tasks. ServiceNow even markets GRC as resolving "issues before they become audit findings," treating finding as a downstream state of an issue rather than a distinct upstream object.
- **OneTrust** — audit and compliance gaps are tracked as risk/issue records routed to remediation; no separately sold finding master.

Catalog implication: the current single `audit_issues` object matches the ServiceNow/OneTrust merge model and is defensible as the catalog's primary cross-domain remediation entity. If the catalog wants to mirror the audit specialists (AuditBoard, Workiva, Archer), a distinct **audit_findings/observations** object (audit-program-scoped, upstream of audit_issues) is the gap to add — it is a Specialist-class entity, not Core, so it is an enhancement rather than a correctness fix.
