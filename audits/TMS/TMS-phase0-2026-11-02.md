# TMS Phase 0 - Vendor Surface (2026-11-02)

Transportation Management System, shipper side: software a company that buys freight uses to source carriers, plan and tender shipments, track them, and reconcile the carrier's charges. This report is research only. It decides nothing and loads nothing. Carrier-side products are covered by the sibling TRUCKING-TMS Phase 0; they appear here only where they are evidence for the boundary.

**How to read the evidence.** In the surface matrix, `X` = confirmed in product docs, API reference or help center. `m` = marketing page only. Blank = not found. Blank does not mean absent, and no cell was filled by inference. Sources carry a type tag in the [Source register](#source-register): `docs`, `vendor` (vendor-authored), `sponsored`, `trade` (trade press), `analyst` (Gartner or Berg, usually read through a vendor reprint or summary), `publisher` (market-report publisher), `aggregator`, `filing` (SEC filing), `law`. Vendor-authored, sponsored and aggregator sources are not counted as independent evidence.

**Notes on specific rows.**
- `hazmat_shipping_papers`: no public surface models this as its own record. Vendors carry hazmat fields on line items (Shipwell line items, Freightview `ExternalHazardousDetail`, `ApiEmergencyContactDTO`) and print through the bill of lading. Oracle docs (search excerpt, page not opened) block bill of lading printing when a hazmat check fails. It loads under the Compliance rule, but a Phase A reviewer could reasonably make it a typed flavor of `bills_of_lading` instead.
- `freight_routing_guides`: Specialist by evidence only. Oracle's nearest resources (`itineraries`, `routingnetworks`, `routingconstraints`) are not the same concept and were not scored. Gartner's TMS definition includes carrier selection, so treat this as under-evidenced rather than rare.

- `freight_carrier_qualifications`: the only Core row with no confirmed cell. Do not treat it as confirmed until a doc is found.

## Compliance entities

The protocol's compliance table has no row for this market. Several of these need a judgment on who actually carries the duty, because the shipper does not always hold the legal obligation that its TMS appears to fulfill.

| Entity | Regulation | Who carries the duty | Proposed owner | Rule #16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bills_of_lading | 49 USC 14706(a); 49 CFR 373.101 | **The carrier** must issue the receipt or bill of lading, and failing to issue one does not affect carrier liability. Vendors nonetheless generate it on the shipper side (Oracle, Shipwell, Freightview), and the shipper's written declaration of value is what can limit liability (14706(c)). The shipper's record is evidence for a claim, not the statutory instrument. | TMS (`master`) | `true` (class B, jurisdiction-bound: not found on Transporeon's public surface, where `consignment_notes` carries the role). Effectively required for US tenants. |
| freight_claims | 49 USC 14706(e): carrier cannot set a filing period under 9 months or a suit period under 2 years. 49 CFR Part 370: a claim must identify the shipment, assert liability, and demand a determinable amount. | **Shipper** files; carrier disposes. ## Modularization hypothesis

**TMS-PROCUREMENT** (master: `freight_bid_events`, with `freight_bids`, `freight_lane_awards`). Strategic carrier sourcing. Transporeon exposes it as a separate API, Trimble sells a "strategic procurement bundle," Manhattan names Transportation Procurement, and Alpega has TenderEasy and an individually deployable module. Gartner's TMS definition opens with bid management. Weakest module by count: confirmed in Transporeon only. Fold candidate into TMS-CARRIER-RATES if the q-file prefers fewer modules.

**TMS-CARRIER-RATES** (masters: `freight_carriers`, `freight_agreements`, `freight_rates`, `freight_lanes`, with `freight_accessorials`, `freight_carrier_relationships`, `freight_capacity_commitments`, `freight_routing_guides`, and carrier qualification). Commercial reference that execution reads. Transporeon has a separate Rate Management API and master-data API; Trimble sells rate management a la carte; Shipwell groups "Carriers and Contracts."

**TMS-SHIPMENTS** (masters: `transportation_orders`, `freight_shipments`, `bills_of_lading`, with releases, stops, handling units, load plans, tenders, quotes, tracking events, documents, and the compliance cluster). Order to delivery. Every vendor with open docs has this; it is the only part Freightview shares fully. I deliberately did not split planning from execution: `freight_shipments` and `freight_tenders` fit both equally, which the protocol treats as a signal to merge. Planning is mostly optimization behavior, not records. The trade-off is real: Oracle lists Operational Planning as a subproduct, and enterprise vendors differentiate there, so a tenant cannot adopt planning as a separately installable module under this split.

**TMS-SETTLEMENT** (masters: `freight_invoices`, `freight_claims`, with exceptions, disputes, audit rules, cost allocations, accruals, vouchers). Audit through approval, stopping at AP. Infios, Shipwell, Transporeon and Trimble all surface this separately, and even Freightview includes it. Ends at a handoff to AP, because Manhattan ("payment to financial systems"), Freightview (exported to accounting), SAP (draft invoices to ERP) and Alpega (settlement documents to SAP) stop there too.

**TMS-DOCK** (masters: `dock_appointments`, `facility_docks`). Optional module for shippers scheduling their own facilities. Separate API at Shipwell, separate product family at Transporeon, separate module at Alpega, Appointment Scheduling at Manhattan. Bound tightly at appointments: gate, trailer moves and yard visits belong to a yard candidate. Absent at Freightview and not found at Infios, so install-optional.

Rule #14 holds: every module has at least one master, and the domain clears the two-module floor at five.

## Per-seam verdicts (for the q-file)

### 1. Point-solution test: passes, `established_market`

**What vendors do.** Independent vendors whose flagship product is a shipper TMS: **Shipwell** (sixth consecutive Gartner appearance, primarily for shippers), **Alpega** (Gartner Challenger, TMS plus freight exchanges, describes itself as a TMS pure-play, which is a vendor claim), **FreightWise** (Kuebix), and **Freightview** (ownership unverified). Further candidates rest on the aggregator's list only: Tesisquare, BlueRock TMS, TMSfirst, Pando. Gartner has run this Magic Quadrant for at least 19 editions (Oracle and Blue Yonder each cite 19th) and Berg Insight is on its fourth edition.

**Recommendation.** Passes. `established_market`, with Shipwell and Alpega as `primary` coverage.

**Trade-off.** The bar clears on smaller vendors, not the market's largest. No Gartner Leader is a flagship-TMS independent: Oracle and SAP are suites, Manhattan is also a WMS leader, and Blue Yonder and e2open sell broader supply chain portfolios (general knowledge, not re-verified here). Consolidation is eating the independents: 3Gtms, MercuryGate, Transporeon, BluJay and Transplace were all absorbed. If "flagship and independent at Gartner scale" were the test, only Shipwell and Alpega are confirmed, which is two, plus unconfirmed Pando.

### 2. Relationship to TRUCKING-TMS: two markets, siblings

**What vendors do.**
- **Trimble** is the clearest case. It sells TMW.Suite and TruckMate to carriers, and in June 2026 launched a separate TMS for Shippers built from Transporeon, described as connecting to and coexisting with the carrier product. One vendor, two products.
- **Oracle** runs both views on one platform and documents why they differ: "A buyer of transportation is anyone who needs to purchase transportation," and "sellers are often carriers." Buy and sell shipments have different visibility, storage domains, rates and settlement (customer billing versus service provider freight payment).
- **Infios** markets one brand to shippers, 3PLs, brokers and carriers, but this is marketing only. It is the strongest counterexample and I could not test it against docs.
- **Manhattan** lists trucking and carrier services among its industries and has Dispatch and Fleet Management (marketing), which fits private fleets at least as well.
- **Shipwell** is primarily for shippers; its carrier-facing surface is portals, though its API also has customer relationships and accounting invoices, which suggests some 3PL use. **Transporeon** connects carriers as counterparties to a shipper's process, through a separate Carrier Interface. **McLeod** sells PowerBroker to brokers (TT News) and LoadMaster to carriers (low-quality comparison site only), with no shipper product found.
- **Analysts disagree on the boundary.** Gartner's definition centers on what TMS gives "a shipper," and McLeod and Trimble TMW are absent from its 2026 list (aggregator, unconfirmed). Berg Insight counts all of it as one TMS market and names Trimble Transportation and McLeod as the most notable North American players.

**Recommendation.** Sibling, not umbrella. Distinct `data_objects` under the buyer-side and seller-side rule, matching the established `invoices` and `customer_invoices` pair. Oracle's documented split is direct vendor support for that shape. Shared: reference data (SCAC, NMFC, hazmat classes, equipment types) and the EDI messages that connect the two parties (204, 990, 214, 210). A tender crosses between two companies' systems; it is not a shared record.

**Trade-off.** Siblings double-model similar shapes, and a private-fleet shipper or asset-based 3PL straddles both with no home for the record that crosses over. Berg's single number also means market size does not split cleanly. An umbrella would reflect Berg and Infios, but it would put a parent over two buyers who, per Trimble's own product line, buy different products. **Open item:** `bills_of_lading` is one legal document held by both parties, so the sibling should coordinate on whether the carrier side uses a prefixed name.

### 5. Module split

**How vendors package it.** See the packaging table. The boundaries that recur: procurement, rates, shipment execution, settlement, dock. Transporeon is the sharpest, with separate APIs for each and a Dock and Yard product family. Trimble says customers can buy a la carte or the full system. Alpega deploys modules individually. Shipwell publishes separate API references for Orders, Shipment Assembly, Freight Pay/Audit and Dock Scheduling. Gartner's definition lists five functions: bid management, shipment planning, carrier execution, track and trace, charge matching. Oracle is the outlier, packaging by planning, fleet and modeling subproducts, with settlement in the base API.

**Recommendation.** The five modules above. Merge planning into shipments. Keep settlement separate. Keep dock optional.

**Trade-off.** Procurement and carrier-rates could merge: only Transporeon confirms the procurement objects, and Trimble and Manhattan treat procurement and rate or contract work as near neighbors. The split is a soft seam, preserved because the bid-event lifecycle ends where the agreement's begins. Separately, Oracle and enterprise vendors would package planning as its own product, so a merged TMS-SHIPMENTS is closer to how mid-market products package it than how Leaders do. Given the stated mid-market focus I lean that way, but it is a real choice.

### 6. Who buys it

**Evidence.**
- **Smallest realistic buyer.** A shipper of roughly 50 shipments a month. Prices are from listings, not vendor quotes: Kuebix OnKue from $69 a month (trade press), Freightview from $99 or $149 (two listings that disagree), MyCarrierTMS free entry plan, ShipperGuide TMS $750 a month (RFP.wiki says unpublished). These are mostly rate, book and track tools. Freightview's spec has no orders, contracts or dock objects.
- **Mid-market definition.** Gartner's Klappich (2015): mid-sized is $25M to $100M annual freight spend, large is above that. He called $25M to $75M "particularly ripe," with 10% to 15% US adoption against above 50% for large shippers.
- **Adoption by size.** Gartner's De Muynck (January 2017): about 10% small, 25% medium, 50% large, with 15% to 20% growth in small and mid-sized use. The same numbers reappear in a 2020 VC blog and a 2022 sponsored Uber Freight piece, which are not independent confirmation. **I found no current figure.**
- **Real mid-market products.** Shipwell: 68% of new logos in 2021 were mid-market, with customers averaging above $100M revenue (vendor-authored, old). Descartes 3G: "mid-to-large" shippers per a deal summary, mid-market per ERP Research. Alpega: "modular, entry-level options" (vendor), mid-market and enterprise per ERP Research. Trimble: a la carte, with no size claim. Gartner evaluates at least Shipwell and Alpega.
- **Against.** FreightWaves Ratings (2024, methodology undisclosed) labels no provider mid-market. GMI gives large enterprises 68% share in 2025, with SMEs fastest-growing (publisher). 3PL-run TMS (Uber Freight, C.H. Robinson are in Gartner's Challengers) may absorb mid-market demand; that is my inference, not sourced.

**Recommendation.** A real mid-market tier exists, not just enterprise plus small-business tools, and it is the tier these vendors are competing for. Three tiers: enterprise (Gartner Leaders), mid-market (Shipwell, Descartes 3G, Alpega, Trimble for Shippers, possibly Infios), small shipper (Freightview, FreightWise, MyCarrier, ShipperGuide). Metadata hypothesis: `min_org_size` = `20 s <500`, `cost_band` = `$$$`, `certification_required` = false.

**Trade-off.** Vendors and Gartner segment by freight spend, and I found nothing mapping that to headcount, so `min_org_size` is an approximation. `20 s <500` fits if the field means the smallest buyer of any tier. If it means a buyer of the full surface in this report, `30 m <2500` is more honest. The adoption data is nearly a decade old.
