## Flagship vendors

EAM (also marketed as "Asset Performance Management" / "Maintenance Management" / CMMS at the lighter end) is the software market for **maintaining physical plant, equipment, fleet, and linear/infrastructure assets** in asset-intensive industries: manufacturing, utilities, oil & gas, mining, transportation, water/wastewater, facilities-as-plant. It is **not** IT asset management. The buyer is a maintenance/reliability/operations org (a plant maintenance manager, reliability engineer, facilities-engineering lead), not an IT asset/SAM manager.

**Market confirmation — EAM buyers ≠ ITAM buyers.** Flagship EAM vendors (Maximo, Hexagon, IFS, Fiix, eMaint) sell to maintenance/reliability/operations leaders in plant, utility, and fleet environments, priced and scoped around physical equipment uptime, preventive maintenance, parts inventory, and safety/regulatory compliance (OSHA, EPA, FDA for regulated manufacturing). ITAM vendors (Flexera, Ivanti, ServiceNow ITAM/SAM, Snow, USU) sell to IT/procurement/finance around software license compliance, hardware refresh, SaaS spend, and cloud cost. Different buyer, different budget line, different compliance frame, different data model. The only shared word is "asset"; the markets do not overlap.

## Modularization hypothesis (Rule #14)

A built-out EAM has ≥3 capabilities, so it needs **≥2 full modules**. The natural decomposition is 6–7 modules, each with ≥1 master:

1. **asset-registry** — masters `assets` (industrial_assets), `locations` (embedded), `asset_specifications`, `service_contracts`; junctions `asset_move_history`. The system-of-record for the physical asset hierarchy and classification.
2. **work-management** — masters `work_orders`, `work_requests`, `crews`/`labor`; junctions `work_order_status_history`, `work_order_labor_records`. The reactive/corrective work lifecycle (operational_workflow; needs lifecycle states).
3. **preventive-maintenance** — masters `preventive_maintenance_schedules`, `maintenance_plans`, `job_plans` (catalog). Scheduled/calendar/usage-triggered PM generation.
4. **condition-monitoring** — masters `asset_meters`, `meter_readings`, `condition_monitoring_readings`. Meter/sensor-driven (condition-based) maintenance triggering.
5. **maintenance-inventory** — masters `spare_parts_inventory`, `item_masters`/`parts`, `purchase_orders`/`requisitions`, `inventory_stockrooms`. MRO parts and procurement tied to work orders.
6. **safety-compliance** — masters `permits_to_work`, `inspection_records`; carries `lockout_tagout_procedures`, `calibration_records`, `emissions_monitoring_records`. Regulatory/EHS substrate.
7. **reliability-analysis** (optional, candidate) — `failure_codes`/`fault_causes` (catalog), `failure_records`, `condition_assessments`. RCA/APM tier; may merge into condition-monitoring if thin.

If two of these (e.g. preventive-maintenance + condition-monitoring) prove thin in Phase A, they merge into a single **maintenance-planning** module — that ambiguity is the protocol's expected "merge signal".

## Market-shape decisions (feeds the q-file)

### 5(a) PARENTING — RECOMMENDATION: **re-parent EAM OUT from under ITAM. Re-parent under REAL-EST (id 141) is the second-best option; the cleaner answer is a NEW top-level physical-asset umbrella, with EAM promoted to top-level if no umbrella is created.**

EAM is currently `parent_domain_id=3` (ITAM, "Umbrella covering hardware, software, cloud-cost, and **enterprise-asset management**"). That parenting reflects a naming coincidence ("asset"), not a market relationship. The point-solution test (Rule #2) settles it: name three independent EAM vendors whose flagship is industrial-equipment maintenance (Maximo, Hexagon, IFS, Fiix, eMaint — easily five) and three ITAM vendors (Flexera, Ivanti, Snow/USU). **These are disjoint vendor sets selling to disjoint buyers.** No EAM vendor competes in software-license compliance; no ITAM vendor competes in plant maintenance work orders. A parent/child edge implies the child is a market-tier or sub-feature of the parent's market — false here.

Grounding in what vendors sell:
- ITAM (Flexera, Ivanti, ServiceNow SAM): software entitlements, license reconciliation, SaaS spend, hardware refresh — IT/finance/procurement buyer.
- EAM (Maximo, Hexagon, IFS, Fiix): equipment uptime, preventive maintenance, MRO spare parts, OSHA/EPA safety compliance — maintenance/reliability/operations buyer.

Candidate new parents, compared:
- **REAL-EST (141)** — "Real Estate and Workplace Management" umbrella over IWMS/CAFM. CAFM/IWMS do facilities maintenance of buildings and space; EAM does maintenance of plant/equipment/fleet. There is genuine adjacency (both are physical-asset maintenance; IWMS suites like Planon/Archibus include a CMMS-lite work-order layer), and re-parenting EAM under REAL-EST groups all physical-asset-maintenance markets together. **But REAL-EST is framed as corporate-real-estate/workplace**, and EAM's industrial plant/utility/fleet scope is broader than buildings. So REAL-EST is a defensible home but a slightly narrow umbrella name for it.
- **A new top-level "Physical Asset Management" / "Asset & Facilities Operations" umbrella** parenting {EAM, CAFM, IWMS, possibly FSM} is the cleanest model: one umbrella for maintaining physical things, parallel to ITAM (maintaining IT things). This is the recommendation if the user is willing to create/rename an umbrella.
- **Top-level (parent_domain_id=null)**, like FSM (31), is the safe minimal move if no umbrella restructuring is wanted: it simply removes the wrong ITAM edge.

Net: **the ITAM edge is wrong and should be removed.** Preferred target = new/renamed physical-asset umbrella (or REAL-EST 141); fallback = top-level. **This is a `b2`-class re-parenting decision — surfaced for user approval, not executed in Phase 0.** (No PATCH performed.)

CAFM/IWMS/FSM overlap note (not a merge): EAM ≠ CAFM (CAFM maintains buildings/space, not production equipment), EAM ≠ FSM (FSM dispatches technicians to customer sites; EAM maintains the owner's own assets). Distinct markets with adjacency, not duplicates. No split/merge proposed.

### 5(b) BUILD vs LEAVE-STUB — RECOMMENDATION: **LEAVE AS A MARKET STUB for this engagement (fix only the structural floor + the parenting edge). Do NOT build out modules/capabilities/system skill now.**

EAM is a large, real market ($3B+ US TAM; Maximo/Hexagon/IFS are Gartner leaders) and absolutely earns its `domains` row. But the build-out question is about *this engagement's buyer*. The engagement targets the **50–250-employee IT-suite buyer** (ITSM/ITAM/SAM/CMDB-adjacent). EAM's buyer is the asset-intensive enterprise: a plant maintenance org with `cost_band=$$$$`, `min_org_size` realistically `40 l <10000` for true Maximo/Hexagon/IFS deployments, certification-heavy (OSHA/EPA/FDA), and a fundamentally different persona. Grounding: Maximo and Hexagon EAM are sold to utilities, refineries, mines, and rail operators — not to a 200-person SaaS company. The CMMS lite tier (Fiix/eMaint) reaches mid-market, but even that is a maintenance-ops product, not an IT-suite product.

So: building EAM's 6–7 modules + capabilities + system skill would add substantial surface that **no target buyer in this engagement consumes**, while the genuinely overlapping facilities-maintenance need (if any) is better served by CAFM. Recommended minimal action set (all non-destructive, additive):
1. **Re-parent** per 5(a) (a `b2` decision — get approval, then PATCH `parent_domain_id`). This is the one edit worth doing regardless of build-out.
2. **Tighten the stub**: the description is fine and vendor-neutral; metadata is mostly sound. Optionally correct `min_org_size` to `40 l <10000` (see 5(c)) — a `b2`-class overwrite of a non-empty buyer-shaped field, so user-approval-gated (Rule #20).
3. **Defer full build-out** (modules/capabilities/skill) to a future dedicated EAM/industrial engagement. Record the modularization hypothesis (above) as the Phase-0 input for whenever that happens.

Note the Rule #14 tension: a `domains` row with zero modules **fails the M1 audit check**. The honest options are (i) build the minimum one full module (asset-registry, which the existing masters already support) to clear M1, or (ii) accept EAM as a knowingly-incomplete stub flagged out-of-scope for this engagement. Recommendation: **(ii) — leave flagged**, since a half-built single module on an out-of-scope industrial market is worse than an explicitly-deferred stub. If the user wants M1 green, build only **asset-registry** (the masters exist) and stop.

## Proposed capabilities (5–8, for a built-out EAM)

1. `asset-lifecycle-tracking` — register, classify, locate, transfer, and retire physical assets through their service life.
2. `work-order-management` — create, schedule, dispatch, execute, and close corrective/reactive maintenance work.
3. `preventive-maintenance-scheduling` — generate calendar- and usage-triggered PM work from plans and job templates.
4. `condition-based-maintenance` — trigger maintenance from meter readings and condition-monitoring thresholds.
5. `mro-inventory-management` — manage spare-parts stock, stockrooms, reservations, reorder points, and procurement tied to work.
6. `maintenance-safety-compliance` — enforce permits-to-work, lockout/tagout, inspections, and regulatory EHS records.
7. `asset-reliability-analytics` — failure coding, RCA, and reliability metrics (MTBF/MTTR/availability).
8. `maintenance-cost-management` — track labor, parts, and contractor cost against assets and work orders for maintenance budgeting.

(Capability names are placeholders pending the canonical `capabilities.capability_code` convention; vendor-neutral per Rule #18.)
