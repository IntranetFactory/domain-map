# SOAR Phase 0 Vendor-Surface Research

Date: 2026-06-13
Scope: Security Orchestration, Automation and Response (SOAR) software market.
Purpose: Decide how to modularize SOAR for the domain-map catalog. Read-only research, no database writes.

## 1. Flagship vendors and canonical schema posture

- **Splunk SOAR (formerly Phantom)**: alert/event-centric. The headline objects are the Container (a normalized event/alert) and the Artifact (the structured data inside a container). Playbooks fire against containers. Schema posture: alert-centric, playbook-as-reaction.
- **Palo Alto Cortex XSOAR (formerly Demisto)**: playbook/incident-centric. The Incident is the spine; Playbooks attach to incident types and drive a War Room investigation. A large Marketplace of Content Packs ships integrations, playbooks, and layouts as versioned, installable units. Schema posture: incident-centric with a first-class content/integration catalog.
- **Tines**: workflow-DAG / no-code automation. The headline object is the Story (a directed graph of Actions). There is no native incident or case object in the core product; Tines added a lightweight Cases capability later, but the canonical master is the Story. Schema posture: workflow-DAG-centric.
- **Torq**: workflow-DAG hyperautomation. Headline object is the Workflow (a DAG of Steps), with Torq adding a Case Management surface in its HyperSOC offering. Schema posture: workflow-DAG-centric, with bolt-on case management.
- **Swimlane Turbine**: low-code security automation. Headline objects are the Playbook (low-code canvas) plus Records/Applications (a flexible, user-defined case-record model) and Solutions. Schema posture: playbook-centric with a configurable record/case layer.
- **ServiceNow Security Incident Response (SIR)**: suite-aligned incumbent. The Security Incident is a record type inside the ServiceNow platform; orchestration is delivered through Flow Designer/IntegrationHub and Playbooks are guided-task flows on top of the platform's task/case model. Schema posture: incident/case-centric, SOAR bundled into ITSM/SecOps.
- **ThreatConnect (optional)**: TIP-hybrid. Headline objects are Threat Intelligence (Indicators, Groups) plus Cases (Workflow) and Playbooks. Schema posture: intelligence-centric with first-class Cases and Playbooks.

## 3. Compliance entities for this market

SOAR is increasingly the system of record for the audit trail that regulators care about, but the compliance objects are generally **derived/reported surfaces, not separate first-class masters** in the flagship products. Concretely:

- **SEC 8-K cyber-incident disclosure trail (Item 1.05, materiality determination within four business days)**: No flagship vendor ships a first-class `sec_8k_disclosure` entity. The audit trail is assembled from the incident timeline, case notes, and run logs. XSOAR and ServiceNow SIR are the two that come closest, because their incident timeline plus immutable audit log plus task/approval records can be exported as the materiality-decision evidence. So: a **materiality_determination** / disclosure-decision record is at most a **Compliance** add-on field set on the incident, not its own master.
- **Breach-notification evidence (GDPR Art. 33/34 72-hour clock, US state breach laws, HIPAA)**: Again handled as incident metadata plus task SLAs and notification tasks, not a dedicated entity. ServiceNow SIR has the strongest native posture here via SLA timers and regulatory task templates.

Net: the only compliance-grade objects that are genuinely first-class anywhere are the **immutable audit log** (run_logs / activity stream, present everywhere as Common) and **case_timelines** (Core in XSOAR and ServiceNow). For the catalog, treat 8-K disclosure and breach-notification as **Compliance-class field sets/tasks layered on security_incidents**, plus an audit-log entity, rather than new masters.

## 4. Modularization hypothesis

Recommended split: keep three modules but **rebalance the boundaries**, with connectors and content treated as an authoring-surface sub-cluster, and case-vs-incident resolved by making the incident the headline case object.

### Question A: Is the security CASE/INCIDENT a first-class entity, distinct from automation runs?

Yes, but the vendors split sharply, and **almost none of them treat "case" and "incident" as two separate masters**:

- **XSOAR**: the **Incident** is the single first-class master. There is no separate "case" table; what you would call a case is an Incident plus its layout, Evidence Board, War Room, and tasks. Case = Incident here.
- **ServiceNow SIR**: the **Security Incident** is a first-class record on the platform task/case model. ServiceNow does have a generic Case construct (CSM), but for SecOps the Security Incident is the spine. Case effectively = Security Incident.
- **Splunk SOAR**: folds everything into the **Container**, which doubles as the incident. No distinct case object.
- **Tines**: no native incident; **deferred to external ITSM** historically, with a later lightweight Cases add-on. Tines is the clearest "defer the case to Jira/ServiceNow" vendor.
- **Torq**: workflow-first, with cases only in the **HyperSOC** add-on. Case is specialist/edition-gated.
- **Swimlane**: the **Record/Application** model is the case, and it is first-class and highly configurable. Swimlane is the one vendor where the case-record layer is arguably the headline.

Verdict A: The case/incident object is first-class and is distinct from automation runs (you never want runs and incidents in one table). But **"security_cases" and "security_incidents" as two separate masters is not what the market does.** The dominant pattern (XSOAR, ServiceNow, Splunk) is one record type that is both. Tines/Torq defer it entirely.

### Question B: Does integration_connectors warrant its own module/surface?

Connectors are a **first-class authored surface with their own lifecycle**, not mere config:

- **Splunk SOAR Apps**: independently versioned, installed/updated, with their own action catalog. A first-class catalog.
- **Cortex XSOAR Marketplace Content Packs**: the strongest case. Integrations ship as versioned, dependency-managed, separately-licensed packs with their own update/deprecation lifecycle. This is unambiguously a first-class authored surface, effectively a software supply chain.
- **Torq integrations** and **Swimlane Connectors**: catalogs of versioned integrations.
- **ServiceNow Spokes/IntegrationHub**: integrations are packaged store apps.
- **Tines**: the exception, integrations are mostly generic HTTP actions plus templated Stories, so the connector catalog is thinner.

Verdict B: integration_connectors belongs in the **authoring module** as a first-class entity (with playbook_versions/content_pack), not in runtime and not as a loose attribute on playbooks. It does not need its own top-level module, because in five of seven vendors it is packaged and versioned alongside playbooks/content (XSOAR Content Packs literally bundle integrations + playbooks + layouts together). Co-locate connectors with playbook authoring.

### Question C: alert/event-centric vs playbook/incident-centric vs workflow-DAG-centric, and what is the headline master?

The market is genuinely three-shaped, which is the core finding:

- **Alert/event-centric (Splunk)**: headline master is the **Container/Artifact**; playbook is a reaction.
- **Playbook/incident-centric (XSOAR, ServiceNow SIR, ThreatConnect, Swimlane)**: headline master is the **Incident/Case**, and the playbook is authored against incident types.
- **Workflow-DAG-centric (Tines, Torq)**: headline master is the **Story/Workflow**, a standalone automation graph with no required incident.

Verdict C: There is no single canonical master across the market. For a neutral catalog, model **two headline masters**: a **playbook/workflow** master (covers Splunk Playbook, XSOAR Playbook, Tines Story, Torq Workflow, Swimlane Playbook) and an **incident/case** master (covers XSOAR Incident, ServiceNow Security Incident, Splunk Container-as-incident, Swimlane Record). Name the authoring master generically (security_playbooks with a workflow/story alias) so the DAG-centric vendors are not second-class.

### Question D: Should security_cases master in a third module, or fold into security_incidents?

**Fold case into incident.** The evidence is decisive:

- XSOAR, ServiceNow SIR, and Splunk all use **one record** that serves as both case and incident. None of the three ships a separate case table.
- Swimlane's case-record is a single configurable Record/Application, not a case-distinct-from-incident pair.
- The only vendors with a literal separate "Cases" object (Tines Cases, Torq HyperSOC cases, ThreatConnect Cases) treat it as the *primary* investigative container, i.e. it plays the incident role, not a second tier below an incident.

Verdict D: Do **not** create both security_cases and security_incidents as separate masters. Make **security_incidents the single case/incident master** (label it to make clear it covers "case"), and keep case_tasks / case_evidence / case_timelines / case_notes as its children. This collapses the proposed third module's two top entities into one.

### Resulting recommended module split

1. **SOAR-PLAYBOOK-AUTHORING** (design-time surface): security_playbooks/workflows, playbook_steps, playbook_versions, integration_connectors, plus content_pack/marketplace_pack, incident_types/layouts, and credentials/vault. This absorbs connectors and content as first-class authored entities (Verdict B).
2. **SOAR-AUTOMATION-RUNTIME** (run-time surface): automation_runs, automation_run_steps, automation_artifacts, automation_run_logs. Keep separate from authoring because every vendor separates a Playbook from its executions, and the run/log volume profile is totally different.
3. **SOAR-CASE-MGMT** (investigation surface): security_incidents (the single case+incident master, Verdict D), case_tasks, case_evidence, case_timelines, case_notes, indicators_of_compromise, plus an audit_log/disclosure compliance field set (Verdict A, Section 3). Drop the separate security_cases entity.

The three-module eyeball split **mostly holds**, with two concrete corrections: (1) connectors live in authoring, not as their own module and not as loose playbook config; (2) security_cases folds into security_incidents, so module 3 has one master, not two.

## 5. Bottom-line recommendation (paste into decision file)

Build SOAR as **three modules**, but correct the boundaries the market actually draws. Module 1 SOAR-PLAYBOOK-AUTHORING owns the design-time surface: a generically named playbook/workflow master (covering Splunk Playbooks, Cortex XSOAR Playbooks, Tines Stories, Torq Workflows, Swimlane Playbooks), its steps and versions, and integration_connectors as a first-class versioned catalog, because XSOAR Marketplace Content Packs, Splunk Apps, Torq and Swimlane integration catalogs all ship connectors as independently versioned, lifecycle-managed authored units rather than playbook config. Module 2 SOAR-AUTOMATION-RUNTIME owns runs, run-steps, artifacts, and immutable run logs, kept distinct because every vendor separates a playbook from its executions. Module 3 SOAR-CASE-MGMT owns a single security_incidents master (do not also create security_cases): XSOAR's Incident, ServiceNow SIR's Security Incident, and Splunk's Container all serve as both case and incident in one record, and the few vendors with a literal Cases object (Tines, Torq HyperSOC, ThreatConnect) use it as the primary investigation container, not a tier below an incident, while Tines and Torq frequently defer the case to an external ITSM entirely. Case tasks, evidence, timelines, notes, and IOCs hang off that incident master, and SEC 8-K and breach-notification trails are modeled as Compliance-class field sets and tasks on the incident plus an audit-log entity, not as new masters.
