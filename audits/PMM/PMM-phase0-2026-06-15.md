# PMM Phase 0 — Vendor Surface

Domain: **PMM — Product Marketing Management**. Status: new-domain candidate, mention count 2 in `audits/_missing-domains.md`, this Phase 0 is the vendor-surface enumeration that precedes Phase A/B drafting.

Scope intent. PMM is promoted as its own domain with a distinct buyer (the Product Marketing lead), separate from Sales Ops / RevOps (GTM-PLAN) and from Product Management (PROD-MGMT). It absorbs:

- **From GTM-PLAN:** the `LAUNCH-ORCHESTRATION` capability (cap id 109) and the drafted-but-unloaded `GTM-PLAN-LAUNCH-ORCH` module (launch plans, launch milestones, readiness gating, battlecards). GTM-PLAN's `B2-S1` decision already names this relocation: no GTM-planning vendor masters launch plans / milestones / checklists / battlecards; Klue, Crayon, Aha! do, with a distinct PMM/PM buyer.
- **From PROD-MGMT:** six PMM-flavored capabilities currently attached to PROD-MGMT (caps 119-124) but realized by no PROD-MGMT module: `LAUNCH-PLANNING` (119), `GTM-LAUNCH-COORDINATION` (120), `MESSAGING-AND-POSITIONING` (121), `SALES-ENABLEMENT-CONTENT` (122), `COMPETITIVE-INTELLIGENCE` (123), `WIN-LOSS-INTERVIEWS` (124). PROD-MGMT's `B2-31-1` / `B2-S1` is the split decision; PROD-MGMT `C1-C2` already records these six as a partial-fail orphan because no PROD-MGMT module hosts them.

Live-catalog verification (read-only): `domains` has no `PMM` row (only `GTM-PLAN` id 104, `PROD-MGMT` id 101). The eight candidate entity names below do not collide with any existing `data_objects` row.

The buyer in every case is the Product Marketing lead.

Notes on classification calls:

- `competitive_intelligence_records` is the headline noun of the CI sub-market; Klue and Crayon are built around it, Aha! carries a lighter "competitor" object. Core.
- `battlecards` is the single most distinctive PMM artifact (the entity GTM-PLAN explicitly could not master). Klue and Crayon make it first-class; Highspot ships it as an enablement "pitch / play" surface. Core.
- `messaging_frameworks` (positioning docs, value-prop canvases, messaging houses) is the widest Core entity (4 of 5): enablement suites and Aha! all manage positioning/messaging artifacts. This is the structural center of the "messaging" sub-market.
- The five launch entities cluster on Aha! Roadmaps Create alone among the flagships, so they read Specialist by the strict >=3-vendor rule. **But the market drives them, not the vendor count**: this is the exact LAUNCH-ORCHESTRATION surface GTM-PLAN already drafted and is relocating here, and Pendo/Productboard/LaunchNotes corroborate launch-plan + readiness management. They load regardless (in-scope by the relocation mandate), and `readiness_checklists` is independently Common (Highspot readiness + Aha!).
- The ten entities named in the task brief are all present; the matrix adds 7 substrate entities (`competitor_profiles`, `intel_sources`, `launch_activities`, `value_propositions`, `content_collections`, `enablement_trainings`, `content_engagement_events`) that the protocol's "workflow substrate" guidance requires so Phase B does not ship headline-noun-only.

## Compliance entities (non-optional)

**None.** PMM is not a regulated market. There are no FCRA / HIPAA / GDPR-localization / SOX / FDA / PCI obligations intrinsic to product-marketing workflows. The one adjacent privacy touch is PII inside `win_loss_interviews` (interviewee names, recorded quotes, call recordings) and inside `content_engagement_events` (buyer contact identity). That is GDPR/CPRA personal-content handling, modeled by setting `has_personal_content = true` on those two `data_objects` (the same pattern PROD-MGMT uses on `customer_feedback_items`), **not** by a dedicated compliance entity or a `domain_regulations` mandatory row. No row in the protocol's compliance table applies. `certification_required = false`.

## Modularization hypothesis

Recommended module count: **four `full` modules.** This is grounded in how the flagships package: the market is genuinely four pure-play sub-markets, each with its own flagship and its own buyer-facing tool category (CI tools, launch-marketing tools, messaging/positioning craft, sales-enablement platforms). Collapsing below four would force unlike masters together (battlecards next to messaging houses next to enablement libraries); going above four would over-fragment a domain that is being newly stood up. Four also satisfies Rule #14 cleanly (>=3 capabilities -> >=2 modules; here 7 capabilities map to 4 modules, every module has >=1 master).

The proposed module set, with the headline master per module:

- **PMM-COMPETITIVE-INTEL** — competitive intelligence and win/loss. Headline master: `competitive_intelligence_records` (with `battlecards`, `competitor_profiles`, `win_loss_interviews`, `intel_sources`). Realizes caps `COMPETITIVE-INTELLIGENCE` (123) and `WIN-LOSS-INTERVIEWS` (124). Flagship anchors: Klue, Crayon.
- **PMM-LAUNCH-MGMT** — go-to-market launch planning and coordination. Headline master: `launch_plans` (with `product_launches`, `launch_milestones`, `readiness_checklists`, `launch_activities`). Realizes caps `LAUNCH-ORCHESTRATION` (109, relocated from GTM-PLAN), `LAUNCH-PLANNING` (119), `GTM-LAUNCH-COORDINATION` (120). Flagship anchor: Aha! Roadmaps Create. This module is the new home of the `GTM-PLAN-LAUNCH-ORCH` surface; GTM-PLAN drops that module and its LAUNCH-ORCHESTRATION capability.
- **PMM-MESSAGING** — messaging, positioning, personas. Headline master: `messaging_frameworks` (with `persona_definitions`, `value_propositions`). Realizes cap `MESSAGING-AND-POSITIONING` (121). Flagship anchors: Seismic, Highspot, Aha! (messaging surfaces).
- **PMM-SALES-ENABLEMENT** — sales-enablement content production and engagement. Headline master: `sales_enablement_content` (with `content_collections`, `enablement_trainings`, `content_engagement_events`). Realizes cap `SALES-ENABLEMENT-CONTENT` (122). Flagship anchors: Highspot, Seismic, Showpad.

`product_launches` vs `launch_plans`: kept as two masters in PMM-LAUNCH-MGMT. `product_launches` is the long-lived launch record (the thing that gets a tier, a GA date, a retro); `launch_plans` is the working plan/playbook instance with milestones and a readiness gate. If Phase A finds they always co-occur 1:1 with no independent lifecycle, collapse to one master then. `messaging_frameworks` and `persona_definitions` sitting together is a deliberate merge signal: positioning craft and persona craft are one buyer workflow, so they share a module rather than splitting PMM-MESSAGING and a thin PMM-PERSONA.

Cross-domain handoffs to wire in Phase A (not loaded here): inbound `product.launch_scheduled` from PROD-MGMT (PM-ROADMAP-DELIVERY) into PMM-LAUNCH-MGMT (PROD-MGMT already owes this outbound, `B9`); inbound `win_loss_interview.completed` / CI feeds toward CRM and REV-INTEL; PMM-SALES-ENABLEMENT consumes CRM opportunity context. GTM-PLAN's `PLAN-TO-EXECUTION-HANDOFF` no longer needs to reach LAUNCH-ORCH internally once that capability relocates.

## Seven domains-row metadata fields (estimated)

For the new `domains` row `PMM` (definitions per references/module-shape.md `domains`):

| Field | Estimate | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `crud_percentage` | **80** | Most of PMM is declarable as CRUD + state workflows + computed fields: launch plans/milestones/readiness gates, messaging docs, persona records, content catalogs, battlecard libraries, win/loss records are all forms-and-workflow. The remainder needing custom code is the analytics/scoring layer (below). Sits between PROD-MGMT (85) and GTM-PLAN (65); PMM is more forms-driven than GTM planning's scenario math, slightly less pure-CRUD than roadmap PM. |
| `business_logic` | **"Competitive-intel relevance scoring and source de-duplication over ingested intel feeds; sales-enablement content engagement analytics and recommendation ranking; win/loss theme extraction and sentiment from interview transcripts; launch-readiness rollup scoring across cross-functional checklists."** | The non-declarative slice: NLP/scoring over CI feeds and win/loss transcripts, content-engagement analytics and ranking, readiness rollup computation. Non-empty because `crud_percentage` is 80 (< 95). |
| `min_org_size` | **`20 s <500`** | PMM as a function appears once a company has a dedicated product-marketer, typically low-hundreds headcount; SMBs buy a CI or enablement point tool well below 500 (Klue/Crayon land in growth-stage software companies). Below PROD-MGMT's `10 xs <50` (tiny teams buy roadmap tools) but below GTM-PLAN's `30 m <2500` (sales planning is mid-market-and-up). |
| `cost_band` | **`$$`** ($25k-$100k for a 500-user org) | Pure-play PMM tools (Klue, Crayon, Aha! Create, Showpad) land in the low-five-to-low-six-figure annual range at this seat count. Enterprise enablement suites (Seismic, Highspot) at full scale can push toward `$$$`, but the 500-user anchor for a product-marketing function keeps the representative band at `$$`. |
| `certification_required` | **`false`** | No product certification or regulator approval gates entry to the product-marketing market; no certified-partner-only delivery requirement. |
| `usa_market_size_usd_m` | **900** | US-only TAM triangulated across the four sub-markets: sales enablement is the largest slice (Highspot/Seismic/Showpad, several hundred $M US), competitive intelligence (Klue/Crayon/Kompyte) a smaller but fast-growing slice, launch-marketing and messaging tooling thinner and partly bundled into PM/roadmap suites. Net US figure lands below PROD-MGMT (1200) and above GTM-PLAN (600). Triangulated from vendor public-revenue disclosures, not a single published analyst line; flag as estimate. |
| `market_size_source_year` | **2024** | Matches the source year used for the sibling GTM-PLAN (600 / 2024) and PROD-MGMT (1200 / 2024) figures for comparability. |

Caveat on market size: there is no clean single Gartner/Forrester "Product Marketing Management" market line; the 900 figure is a triangulation across the four adjacent point-solution markets (CI, sales enablement, launch/roadmap-marketing, messaging tooling) and should be treated as an order-of-magnitude estimate pending a published analyst figure.
