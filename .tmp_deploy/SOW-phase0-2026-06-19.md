# SOW Phase 0 - Vendor Surface (Services Procurement / Statement of Work)

## Point-solution test verdict (Rule #2)

**FAILS the strict >=3 independent pure-play test.** I can name only **one** genuine pure-play and **one** near-pure-play; every other credible vendor bundles SOW inside a VMS or Source-to-Pay (S2P) suite. SOW/services-procurement is therefore best modeled as a **cross-cutting module that is BOTH a thin standalone market AND a bundled tab** - not a deep standalone domain.

1. **Zivio** (https://www.zivio.com/) - GENUINE pure-play. Homepage: "The leading AI-powered Services Procurement System... buy outsourced services, contracted under a Statement of Work." Positions explicitly as "a powerful alternative to traditional Vendor Management Systems (VMS) and sourcing-only platforms." Spend Matters classifies it as a services-sourcing solution. SOW is unambiguously its flagship and its entire revenue.
2. **Globality** (https://www.globality.com/) - NEAR-pure-play, but disqualified as a strict third. Its flagship is "autonomous sourcing" of services/SOWs (Glo AI brief -> supplier match -> auto-filled SOW), historically services-led. But current positioning is "AI-Driven Savings For All Enterprise Spend" across consulting, marketing, IT, HR, legal, even stationery/tail spend. SOW is a strong use case, not the whole product. Counts as the near-third.
3. **No credible independent third.** Every other candidate is a bundler: SimplifyVMS, Beeline, SAP Fieldglass, Workday VNDLY, Randstad, Magnit are all VMS; Coupa, Ivalua, Jaggaer, SAP Ariba are S2P suites. AtriumWORKS / Workspace / nextSource are **MSP managed services** (tech-agnostic, "we partner with world-class VMS/SMS/ERP"), not software products. Closest near-miss is AtriumWORKS but it ships no platform of its own.

So: **1 pure-play + 1 near-pure-play + a wall of bundlers.** This is dual-modeling evidence, not standalone-domain evidence.

## Dual-modeling evidence

SOW is modeled BOTH ways simultaneously, which is the whole point. As a **standalone product** it exists at Zivio (explicit, pure-play) and, loosely, Globality (services-sourcing flagship). As a **bundled module** it appears inside Beeline VMS, SAP Fieldglass Services Procurement, Workday VNDLY SOW, Coupa Services Procurement, and Ivalua SOW-based spend management - in every case a "Services Procurement / SOW" tab grafted onto a contingent-workforce VMS or an S2P suite. The standalone market is real but thin (essentially Zivio + Globality); the bundled footprint is everywhere. That asymmetry is the dual-modeling signal: a single shared entity cluster (`statements_of_work`, `services_engagements`, `sow_milestones`, `service_deliverables`, `milestone_invoices`) consumed by VMS and S2P alike.

## Anti-VMS / anti-S2P positioning quotes

- **Zivio (anti-VMS, verbatim from MSP solution page):** "You will no longer have to force your SOW management processes into a VMS designed for contingent workforce." Reinforced on homepage: "a powerful alternative to traditional Vendor Management Systems (VMS) and sourcing-only platforms." Its argument: VMS was built to control large populations of T&M contractors; services procurement needs separate framework, governance, and platform. (https://www.zivio.com/solutions/msp)
- **Zivio (anti-S2P, softer):** positions as "best-of-breed... standalone or embedded within the S2P stack" - i.e., it concedes it can plug into S2P but insists the SOW logic belongs in a dedicated tool, not buried in the suite. (Spend Matters Zivio vendor analysis)
- **Globality (anti-traditional-RFx/S2P):** "go to market in as little as 23 minutes" vs "traditional RFx processes... up to six months"; frames legacy S2P sourcing as slow and manual, replaced by autonomous AI sourcing. (https://www.globality.com/)

## Master-vs-consume boundaries

- **Services VENDOR:** **Consume `suppliers`, do not master a separate `services_suppliers`.** Zivio and Globality both onboard and risk-assess suppliers but treat them as the same supplier master a buyer already has; the only delta is supplier *performance/scorecard* data, which can hang off the engagement. VMS/S2P bundlers (Fieldglass, Coupa, Ivalua) master suppliers because they ARE the procurement system. For an SOW domain, reference an existing supplier master.
- **The CONTRACT:** **Reference, do not master.** The SOW is a contract artifact, but these tools do NOT replace CLM; they create/negotiate the SOW document and then track delivery against it. Master `statements_of_work` and `services_engagements` as operational records, but link to (not own) any MSA/master-contract record that a CLM domain holds. Coupa's Navi even "analyzes legal contracts" to pre-fill - the legal contract lives elsewhere.
- **RATE CARDS:** **Consume, mostly.** Bundlers (Fieldglass, Beeline, Coupa, Ivalua) master rate cards because they share them with their VMS/timesheet engine. The pure-plays barely surface rate cards (Zivio only partially; Globality not at all) because deliverable/milestone-priced SOW work is outcome-based, not rate-card-based. So `services_rate_cards` is Common-not-Core and should be consumed from VMS/S2P where one exists, not mastered by an SOW domain.

## Compliance / regulatory entities (if any)

Services procurement is lightly regulated, so there is **no rich compliance entity cluster**. The real risk is **contingent/co-employment / misclassification**: an SOW used as disguised staff augmentation (SOW-as-staff-aug) can re-classify the firm's workers as the buyer's contingent labor, triggering co-employment and tax exposure - hence Zivio's insistence on keeping SOW out of contingent-workforce tooling. Worth a single `compliance_flags` or `engagement_classifications` attribute rather than a table. Secondary, US-specific: **1099 reporting** on firm payments and, for public-sector buyers, **FAR / government-services-contracting** clauses (CLIN/SLIN line items, contract mods) - but no surveyed commercial vendor models FAR entities, so treat government-services-contracting as out of scope / a separate public-sector domain.
