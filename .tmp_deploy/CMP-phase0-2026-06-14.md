Candidate: **CMP (Content Marketing Platform)**, surfaced from the triage queue.
Module: domain_map (id 1001), tenant adenin.

## VERDICT: fold-into-existing:WEB-CONTOPS

The editorial-content-operations kernel that defines this candidate (editorial workflow,
content briefs, production calendar, editorial task management, multi-channel publishing
orchestration) is already absorbed by the loaded **WEB-CONTOPS (Web Content Operations &

warranted. Build nothing.

## Two ambiguities resolved up front

### 1. Acronym overload: CMP -> Content Marketing Platform vs Consent Management Platform
"CMP" most often means **Consent Management Platform** (OneTrust, Didomi, Usercentrics,
Sourcepoint, Cookiebot, TrustArc) in a privacy/cookie-compliance market, or **Cloud
Management Platform** (Morpheus, Flexera, CloudBolt). The candidate was surfaced partly by a
**PRIV-MGMT** audit, which strongly suggests that audit meant **Consent Management
Platform**, a genuinely different market with its own pure-play flagships.

This Phase 0 researched **Content Marketing Platform** per the supplied vendor evidence
(Skyword, Contently, NewsCred, Welcome, Optimizely CMP, ContentCal), which is unambiguously
the content-marketing market.

**FLAG for the controller:** "CMP / Consent Management Platform" is a *separate potential
candidate* the PRIV-MGMT audit likely intended. It was NOT researched or built here. It
should be re-triaged on its own (privacy-tech market: OneTrust, Didomi, Usercentrics,
Sourcepoint, TrustArc, Cookiebot), and likely positioned near a privacy / consent / GRC
cluster rather than the marketing cluster. Do not conflate the two under one "CMP" row.

### 2. Category maturity (the content-marketing reading)
- Gartner retired the **Web Content Management (WCM)** Magic Quadrant in June 2020
  ("commoditization of WCM functionality"; demand shifted to DXP). It did **not** retire the
  Content Marketing Platforms MQ in 2020. A CMP MQ continued through 2023, 2024, 2025, and a
  2026 edition exists (Optimizely cites a "9th year in a row" Leader placement in the 2026
  CMP MQ).
- So a *named* CMP category still exists at Gartner. But the surviving entrants are
  overwhelmingly DXP / CMS / broader-suite vendors evaluated in a CMP lens (Optimizely,
  Sitecore, Sprinklr, Storyteq, Adobe), not independent editorial-content-ops pure-plays.
  The category has converged into DXP / CMS / marketing-orchestration suites exactly as
  predicted, even though the MQ label persists.

## Point-solution-market test (honest application)

Question (Rule #2): *can I name three independent pure-play vendors whose flagship product is
an editorial-content-ops Content Marketing Platform, still standing in 2026?*

- **Surviving pure-plays:** Skyword, Contently (both services+platform hybrids, not pure
  SaaS), plus thin SMB players DivvyHQ / CoSchedule.
- **Everything else named in the evidence has folded:** Welcome/NewsCred -> Optimizely;
  ContentCal -> Adobe (discontinued); GatherContent -> Bynder; Kapost -> Upland; Percolate
  -> Seismic; Curata/ScribbleLive/TrackMaven absorbed.
- The "leaders" still evaluated under the CMP label are DXP/CMS/suite vendors (Optimizely,
  Sitecore, Sprinklr, Adobe, Storyteq), already represented by DXP / WEB-CONTOPS / MA.

**Result: FAIL as a standalone domain.** There are at most ~2 genuine independent pure-plays
(Skyword, Contently), both hybrid managed-services businesses, plus a tail of thin SMB
calendar tools. That does not clear the >=3-surviving-flagship bar for an independent
editorial-content-ops market in 2026. The kernel is a *capability cluster*, not a domain.

## Recommendation

1. **Fold into WEB-CONTOPS.** Record CMP (Content Marketing Platform) in the Folded section
   pointing at WEB-CONTOPS. No new domain, no new modules, no new solutions, no rows loaded.
2. WEB-CONTOPS already holds the flagship solutions; if the controller wants, a later
   discretionary (b3) pass could *add* Skyword / Contently / DivvyHQ as `solutions` under
   WEB-CONTOPS (with Skyword/Contently flagged as services+platform hybrids). Non-blocking,
   not part of this fold, and not done here.
3. **Separately re-triage "CMP / Consent Management Platform"** as its own privacy-market
   candidate (the PRIV-MGMT audit's likely true intent).
