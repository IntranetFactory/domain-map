# DAP Phase 0 — Vendor Surface

## Market definition & classification verdict

DAP (Digital Adoption Platform) is a clearly **established_market**: an analyst-tracked category (Gartner runs a "Digital Adoption Platforms" Peer Insights category) with multiple independent pure-play vendors that ship no underlying business application of their own. A DAP injects a guidance layer (walkthroughs, tooltips, smart tips, launchers, resource centers, in-app surveys) over OTHER software and captures product-usage telemetry to drive adoption. At least three independent pure-plays exist: **Whatfix** (private, Warburg Pincus / SoftBank backed), **Userlane** (independent, Munich), and **Spekit** (independent, Denver). WalkMe is now an SAP subsidiary but remains a distinct product line.

There is a real **employee-facing vs customer-facing-PLG split**. Employee-facing DAP (WalkMe, Whatfix, Userlane, Spekit) overlays internal enterprise apps (Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, SAP) to cut training cost and support tickets; buyer is IT / enablement / L&D. Customer-facing product-led-growth onboarding (Userpilot, Appcues, Chameleon) overlays a vendor's OWN SaaS product to drive trial activation, feature adoption, and expansion; buyer is product / growth. The persisted entity surface is ~80% identical (flows, steps, segments, in-app content, surveys, usage events), so this is **one domain with two go-to-market wings**, not two domains. Recommendation: model DAP as a single established_market domain and treat the split as a positioning/segment attribute, not a domain boundary.

## Modularization hypothesis

Three full modules, each with its own master(s), satisfying Rule #14 (>=3 capabilities -> >=2 modules; we propose three).

- **guidance** (the core): authoring and delivery of in-app content. Masters: guidance_flows, smart_tips, launchers, resource_centers, task_lists, audience_segments. Scopes flows/steps, segmentation, versioning, localization, publishing environments, and overlay accessibility conformance.
- **analytics** (product-usage / adoption analytics): masters tracked_pages, tracked_features, end_user_profiles, adoption_goals. Scopes usage_events, funnels, completion, session replays, experiment variants, plus all GDPR/CCPA consent, DSR, and PII-masking compliance.
- **engagement** (in-app voice-of-customer): masters in_app_surveys, announcements, feedback_items. Scopes NPS/CSAT, survey responses, and announcement/feedback capture.

A fourth knowledge-integration cut (help_articles, self-service) is real but thin and folds cleanly into guidance; do not split it out unless drafting reveals enough distinct entities.

## Adjacency / boundary recommendations

- **PROD-MGMT (id 101)**: it owns product *discovery/feedback/roadmap/release* (RICE scoring, feedback clustering, Pendo Roadmaps). DAP analytics owns *adoption telemetry of the guidance layer and tracked features*. Boundary: PROD-MGMT masters feedback-to-roadmap decisions; DAP masters raw usage_events / flow_completion. DAP must NOT re-master roadmaps or feedback prioritization; it may emit feedback_items that PROD-MGMT consumes.
- **DEM (id 83) Digital Experience Monitoring**: owns performance/error/latency of the real user session. DAP captures behavioral adoption events, not synthetic/RUM performance. No overlap in masters.
- **DXP (id 77) Digital Experience Platform**: owns the customer-facing site/portal CMS and delivery. DAP overlays third-party apps; it does not host the page. No master overlap.
- **LMS (id 57) Learning Management**: owns structured courses, enrollments, certifications. DAP delivers contextual in-the-flow-of-work microguidance, not graded curricula. help_articles stays in DAP; formal courseware stays in LMS.
- **KMS (id 33) Knowledge Management**: owns the canonical knowledge base of articles. DAP's help_articles are surfaced in-context and may federate from KMS; KMS remains the system of record for long-form knowledge.
- **EMP-EXP (id 62) Employee Experience**: owns engagement surveys, journeys, sentiment for HR. DAP's in_app_surveys target software-task feedback, not HR engagement. Keep survey masters separate.

## Estimated market metadata (best-effort, for the domains row)

- **US TAM**: ~$650M-$900M US (global DAP ~$1.4B-$1.6B in 2024-2025 per analyst estimates; US is roughly half). Source: vendor revenue triangulation (WalkMe ~$1.5B acquisition, Whatfix >Rs 500 crore FY25) and Gartner DAP category sizing, 2024-2025.
- **min_org_size**: "30 m <2500" band — enterprise-led but PLG wing reaches mid-market SaaS.
- **cost_band**: `$$$$` (enterprise DAP seat/MAU pricing is high; PLG tools cheaper but category skews up).
- **certification_required**: `false`. No statutory certification to operate a DAP; compliance obligations (GDPR/CCPA/WCAG) are configuration duties, not a licensing gate.
- **crud_percentage**: ~45. Heavy authoring/config CRUD on flows and content, balanced against high-volume telemetry ingest and analytics computation that is not CRUD.
