# ITFM / TBM Phase 0 — Vendor Surface Research

Candidate new domain: IT Financial Management / Technology Business Management.
Date: 2026-06-16. Research only — zero inserts, no commit.

---

## Point-solution verdict: YES, it is a domain

ITFM/TBM passes the Rule #2 point-solution-market test cleanly. There is a recognized,
analyst-tracked software category (Gartner "IT Financial Management Tools" Market Guide;
the TBM Council standardizes the TBM taxonomy as an open framework) and multiple
independent pure-play vendors whose flagship product IS this market:

- **Apptio** (now an IBM company, acquired 2023) — the category-defining pure-play. Apptio
  Costing / ApptioOne / Cloudability + IT Planning. Flagship product is whole-IT cost
  transparency, allocation, and budgeting built on the TBM taxonomy.
- **Nicus Software** — independent pure-play ITFM/TBM vendor. M-ITFM platform; cost
  modeling, showback/chargeback, IT budgeting. Sole focus is ITFM.
- **MagicOrange** — independent pure-play cost-allocation/transparency engine (IT and
  shared-services cost allocation). Pure-play.
- **Serviceware** (Serviceware Financial, formerly the SABIO/Serviceware "Performance"
  suite) — European pure-play ITFM vendor; IT cost transparency, planning, allocation.

That is four independent vendors whose flagship product is this exact market — well past the
"name three" bar. A suite anchor reinforces it: **ServiceNow** ships a dedicated module
(ITFM / "IT Finance" / Application Portfolio cost) and **Tangoe** and **Calero** anchor the
adjacent telecom/technology-expense-management (TEM) wing. The category has its own
standards body (TBM Council), its own taxonomy (the TBM taxonomy: Cost Pools → IT Towers →
Solutions/Services → Business Units, the "Bill of IT"), and its own buyer (the IT Finance
Office / IT BMO). All three are domain fingerprints.

Caveat carried forward (segment honesty, see 5(c)): the category is real but it is an
**enterprise** market. It does not serve the 50–250-user segment.

### Engagement / outreach

ITFM/TBM is an analytics-and-allocation market, not a CRM-shaped one. Engagement surface is
thin: `cost_review_notes` (Specialist, ServiceNow-style review threads on a showback
statement) and `approval_requests` for budget sign-off (folded into the
APPROVAL-WORKFLOW cross-cutting module, not mastered here).

### Audit / compliance

No statutory compliance regime is specific to ITFM/TBM (it is internal management
accounting, not a regulated filing). General SOX-style controls (`audit_trails`,
`period_locks`) ride on FIN's close process, not on this domain. See Compliance section.

## Compliance entities (non-optional)

**None specific to this market.** ITFM/TBM is internal management/cost accounting; it
produces no regulator filing and is subject to no market-specific statute. The protocol
compliance table has no row for it. Where ITFM data feeds the audited financial close
(US public co.), the SOX controls (`sox_audit_trails`, `accounting_period_closes`) live on
**FIN**, not here — ITFM is a consumer of the GL, not the system of record. `certification_required`
is therefore FALSE.

## Modularization hypothesis (Rule #14)

Candidate has ≥3 capabilities, so ≥2 full modules required. Proposed three full modules,
each master-bearing:

1. **cost-allocation-modeling** (masters: `it_cost_models`, `cost_pools`, `it_towers`,
   `cost_allocation_rules`, `rate_cards`, `tbm_taxonomies`, `allocation_runs`,
   `allocation_results`, `tbm_taxonomy_mappings`). The engine: the TBM-taxonomy-driven
   model that pushes raw GL/source cost through cost pools and towers into services. This is
   the structurally heaviest cluster (versioned models, run history, methodology config) and
   is the MagicOrange/Apptio core.
2. **cost-transparency-showback** (masters: `it_services`, `service_costs`,
   `technology_business_units`, `consuming_business_units`, `showback_statements`,
   `chargeback_invoices`, `unit_cost_metrics`, `bills_of_it`). The output: per-service,
   per-business-unit cost transparency, the "Bill of IT," showback and chargeback.
3. **it-budgeting-and-planning** (masters: `it_budgets`, `budget_line_items`, `forecasts`,
   `budget_actuals_variances`, `scenario_plans`, `budget_templates`). Plan-vs-actual IT
   budgeting and forward forecasting. Present in Apptio/Nicus/Serviceware/ServiceNow.

Borderline 4th module: **telecom-expense** (`telecom_inventory_items`, `telecom_invoices`,
plus vendor-invoice reconciliation). This is the Tangoe/Calero TEM wing. It has its own
independent pure-play market (Tangoe, Calero, Brightfin, Sakon) and is arguably a **separate
domain (TEM)**, not an ITFM module — see 5(b). Recommendation: do NOT master TEM entities
inside ITFM; flag TEM as its own candidate domain.

## Market-shape decisions

### 5(a) Is ITFM/TBM a distinct domain, a sub-domain of FIN, or a capability cluster?

**Distinct top-level domain.** Grounded in the point-solution test: four independent
pure-plays (Apptio, Nicus, MagicOrange, Serviceware) sell this as their flagship, plus a
standards body (TBM Council) and an analyst category (Gartner ITFM Tools). That is the exact
fingerprint Rule #2 demands. It is NOT a sub-feature of FIN: FIN vendors (NetSuite,
SAP S/4, Workday Financials, Sage Intacct) do not master cost pools, IT towers, the TBM
taxonomy, or the Bill of IT — those are absent from every general-ledger product. ITFM
**consumes** FIN's GL as a cost source and republishes it through an IT-specific allocation
model. Consider modeling `parent_domain_id` only if FINOPS/ITFM/TEM are later grouped under
an "IT cost" umbrella, but on its own merits ITFM is top-level.

### 5(b) Boundaries, by name — what ITFM masters that adjacent domains do NOT

- **vs FINOPS (id 41, Cloud Financial Operations):** FINOPS (Cloudability, CloudHealth,
  Apptio Cloudability, native AWS/Azure cost tools) is **cloud-only** cost visibility and
  optimization — it masters `cloud_cost_records`, rightsizing recommendations, reserved-
  instance/savings-plan coverage, scoped to public-cloud bills. ITFM/TBM is **whole-IT-estate**:
  it allocates the ENTIRE IT spend (data center, network, labor, software, depreciation,
  AND cloud) across business units via the TBM taxonomy. FINOPS optimizes one bill; ITFM
  allocates all of IT. Apptio itself sells both (Cloudability = FINOPS; ApptioOne = ITFM),
  which proves they are distinct surfaces inside one vendor. ITFM masters `cost_pools`,
  `it_towers`, `tbm_taxonomies`, `bills_of_it` — FINOPS masters none of these.
- **vs FIN (id 65, Core Financial Management):** FIN is the **system of record** — general
  ledger, AP/AR, fixed assets, period close, consolidation. ITFM masters NO journal entries,
  NO sub-ledgers, NO statutory financials. ITFM reads FIN's actuals as a `cost_source` and
  produces management-accounting allocations FIN never holds. FIN answers "what did we
  spend, by GL account"; ITFM answers "what did each IT service and each business unit
  consume." `service_costs`, `showback_statements`, `chargeback_invoices` are ITFM, not FIN.
- **vs SPEND-MGMT (id 133, Corporate Spend Management):** SPEND-MGMT masters employee-
  initiated spend at the card/expense/bill-pay layer (Ramp/Brex/Airbase-shaped) — real-time
  card authorization and pre-spend policy. ITFM masters none of that transaction substrate;
  it allocates already-incurred cost. No overlap in mastered entities; the only shared
  concept is `vendor_contracts`, which both embedded-master and FIN/S2P owns canonically.
- **vs ITAM (id 3, IT Asset Management):** ITAM (Flexera One, Ivanti Neurons) masters the
  **asset** and its TCO — hardware/software inventory, lifecycle, and per-asset total cost
  of ownership (the APM/portfolio-reporting wing does asset TCO). ITFM masters the
  **service/business-unit** cost view: it does NOT track individual asset serial numbers,
  warranties, or license entitlements. ITAM answers "what is this asset's lifetime cost";
  ITFM answers "what does the email service cost the Sales business unit this quarter." ITFM
  may consume ITAM's asset cost as a `cost_source`; it does not master `assets`.

### 5(c) Segment honesty — does NOT serve the 50–250-user segment

State plainly: **ITFM/TBM is an enterprise market and does not serve the 50–250-user
segment** this engagement started from. The named evidence: Apptio's, Nicus's, and
Serviceware's customer bases are large IT organizations (thousands of employees, IT budgets
in the tens-to-hundreds of millions); Apptio list pricing and implementation run into six
figures with multi-month TBM-taxonomy modeling engagements. A 50–250-user org has neither
the cost complexity (no internal IT towers, no shared-service chargeback, no business-unit
showback) nor the budget to justify it. **For the mid-market, the "IT budget" need is met by
the general ledger (FIN) plus spreadsheets** — IT cost is a handful of GL lines and a vendor
list, not a modeled allocation. This honesty must be reflected in `min_org_size` (xl) and
`cost_band` ($$$$): the catalog must not imply this market serves SMB/mid-market buyers
(the Rule-#1928 internal-consistency check).

## Proposed capabilities (draft, 5–8)

1. **IT Cost Transparency** — produce per-service and per-business-unit cost views (the Bill of IT) from modeled IT spend.
2. **Cost Allocation Modeling** — define and run driver-based allocation from cost pools through IT towers to services.
3. **Showback and Chargeback** — generate consumption-based statements/invoices to business units (showback) or bill them (chargeback).
4. **IT Budgeting and Forecasting** — build IT budgets, track plan-versus-actual variance, and forecast forward spend.
5. **TBM Taxonomy Management** — map source costs to the standardized cost-pool/tower/service taxonomy and maintain conformance.
6. **Unit Cost and Benchmarking** — compute and trend unit-cost metrics (cost per VM, per mailbox, per ticket) for efficiency analysis.
7. **Cost Scenario Planning** — model what-if scenarios (rate changes, volume shifts, cloud migration) against the cost model.
8. **Cost Data Integration** — ingest and reconcile cost sources (GL actuals, cloud bills, asset costs, labor) into the model.

## Proposed owning business_function (Phase C spine)

**Primary: a new sub-function under Finance — "IT Financial Management"** (parent
`Finance`, id 4), sitting alongside `Financial Planning and Analysis` (id 43) and `Cloud
Financial Operations` (id 83, already under Finance). Rationale: the buyer is the IT Finance
Office / IT Business Management Office, a finance-discipline owner, and the existing spine
already places `Cloud Financial Operations` under Finance — ITFM is the whole-estate sibling
of that cloud-only function, so it belongs in the same place for consistency.

**Secondary contributor: IT Operations** (id 27) — IT leadership consumes the showback/Bill
of IT and owns the IT towers being costed. Model via a `business_function_domains` /
`business_function_capabilities` contributor edge, not as the primary owner.

If a new function is undesirable, the fallback primary is `Financial Planning and Analysis`
(id 43); avoid placing it under IT Operations as primary, since the discipline (allocation,
budgeting, chargeback) is finance, not infrastructure.
